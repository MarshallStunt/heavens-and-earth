Sun path tilt—

—

More about this

How steeply the Sun's path (and the analemma on it) leans: smaller = steeper, a bigger height difference between June and December. 42.5° is the model's default. For comparison, the Sun's real size change through the year implies about 88.3° (only ~100 mi between June and December).

Eclipses —

More about this

Predicted by the tori. The Moon's ring rides 4% above the Sun's layer; at the two nodes, Rahu ☊ and Ketu ☋ (drifting round every 18.6 years), it dips through it when it meets the Sun — a solar eclipse, cast by the 3D Moon. Lunar eclipses come from the shadow body on the hub axis, which always sits on the line from the Sun to the point opposite it. Shadow positions on the map are approximate; the panel shows how far they are from the real ones.

More about this

The real ISS, from live data (NASA's precise trajectory; CelesTrak elements as a backup): exactly over its real position on the map, at its real altitude (257–272 mi, on the scale bar's scale), with a line down to the point beneath it. The station itself is drawn at its true size — 109 m across its solar-array truss, ~73 m of modules — flying as the real one does (modules along the direction of travel). From afar a glowing marker shows where it is; zoom in, or use Follow the ISS, to see the station. Its trail and the Pattern Lab's ISS paths are the real path at the real altitude.
